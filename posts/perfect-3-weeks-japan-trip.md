<h2 data-content='Day 2: Fuji'>Day 2: Fuji</h2>

Kamaiwakan Mount Fuji hut

Climbing Mount Fuji! Most people take a bus to the Fuji-Subaru Line 5th Station, but what I did and highly recommend is to start from the base. I started from my stay, a very nice guesthouse called Matsuyama Base, around 10am. Don’t be like me, starts earlier and bring more water than you are planning, if the weather is hot. I reached the Kitaguchi Hongu Fuji Sengen Shrine quickly. The hike starts here, following an old pilgrim route. I did the first part of the hike without seeing almost no one. The Bear signs got we worried, and without a bear bell, I walked at the sound of the only album I had saved offline on my phone: [_Sonic The Hedgehog DJ Style “Party”_](https://deezer.page.link/6qT8rLixJaj83SwZ8). As you can imagine from the name, not the most appropriate for a outdoor hike but what a great album!

From 900m high to 2900m in 7 hours.

<h2 data-content='Day 3: Typhoon'>Day 3: Typhoon</h2>

Fuji → Kyoto

I hardly woke up at 5am like everyone in the hut, rushing to see the sun rising. Just to have my expectations crushed by a foggy rainy landscape caused by a typhoon.
The guides from the hut said it was too dangerous to go up in this conditions but still asked, so politely, if I wanted to go :D

I was half prepared so the descent was wet, very wet. Once reached Kawaguchiko Station, I took the train to Kyoto. From my first Shinkansen, I could watch Fujisan, or what it wanted to show behind the clouds.
